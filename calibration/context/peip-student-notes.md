# PEIP: our client notes

Team notes for the generic-mode calibration rerun. Written as a student team would, from our own reading; no case text.

**Client.** Prince Edward Island Preserve Company (PEIP), a premium preserves (jam) maker in New Glasgow, PEI, Canada. Founder and owner: Bruce MacNaughton. The company went through receivership once and has a $685K mortgage (interest about $73.7K a year).

**Businesses.**
- Jam: about 80 SKUs, including an organic line. Production runs about five months a year; the kettles are only about half used.
- Non-jam: a café/restaurant at New Glasgow (about 29% margin, taking about 40% of Bruce's time), a garden and a theatre (roughly break-even), and a kiosk in Charlottetown.

**Channels for jam.**
- Retail store at New Glasgow: summer tourists, cruise-ship passengers, bus tours.
- Mail order: catalogues, many US customers; freight is a large share of mail-order sales; FDA labelling rules apply.
- Wholesale distributors in Ontario and the Maritimes.
- Export: a Japanese buyer is interested in large volumes of the organic line.
- Possible: hotels and airlines.

**Problems we heard.**
- Revenue is seasonal and depends on tourists; cash runs short in the off-season.
- Bruce does a lot himself; there is little management depth and the bookkeeping has been unstable.
- The bank wants confidence that PEIP won't fail again.
